# Probabilities, samples, metrics and numbers

32. Do not replace the overall frequency with vivid, specific information; explicitly indicate the base rate and the comparison group.
33. Do not compare numerators without denominators; provide proportions, sample sizes, and observation periods.
34. Do not assume that the intersection of two events is more likely than each of them individually; formally write out the events and their relationships.
35. Do not draw a firm conclusion based on a small, non‑representative, or non‑random sample.
36. Do not take a cluster of observations as a pattern without comparing it with the expected random variation.
37. Do not expect that an independent random process will “compensate” for previous results if the mechanism has not changed.
38. Do not extend a successful series into the future without checking the base frequency, independence, and causal mechanism.
39. Do not forget about the return of extreme observations to the usual level during repeated measurements.
40. Do not compare absolute values without a relative base, and do not compare percentages without an absolute scale.
41. Do not present a point estimate as precise knowledge; show the range, uncertainty, and sensitive assumptions.
42. Do not confuse probability with certainty, and low probability with impossibility.
43. Do not combine incomparable risks, metrics, or periods into a single estimate without an explicit aggregation rule.
44. Do not transfer the measurement result to another population, environment, or period without verifying that the mechanism remains unchanged.
45. Do not optimize the indicator until you have verified that it actually measures the desired outcome, rather than a convenient surrogate.
119. Do not assume that a normal situation cannot change abruptly; check for early warning signs and failure scenarios.
120. Do not interpret interests as a zero‑sum game until you have verified the possibility of mutual benefit.
121. Do not evaluate the sum of parts as the sum of their individual probabilities without checking for overlaps and dependencies.
122. Do not allow the denomination of a banknote, the unit of packaging, or the portion size to change the value without recalculating it based on the total amount.
123. Do not overload the solution with a large number of options; first filter them based on clear criteria.
