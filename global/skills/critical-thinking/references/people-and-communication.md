# Evaluating people and work, groups and understanding

60. Do not transfer the overall impression of a person, brand, project, or team to a single criterion; evaluate the criteria separately.
61. Do not compare an object only with the previous object; use a consistent rubric and several relevant benchmarks.
62. Do not let group affiliation, familiarity, or similarity to yourself change the assessment when the data is the same.
63. Don’t create confirmation of expectations yourself: don’t change access to tasks, support, or the assessment standard due to an early forecast about a person.
64. Don’t assume that others share your opinion or your knowledge; verify this with a question, data, or a retelling.
65. Don’t overstate your own contribution to the overall result; compare your contribution with the shared records and observations of other participants.
66. Don’t ascribe motives, hidden thoughts, or understanding to the other person without explicit confirmation.
67. Don’t assume that the impact of a message is strong only on “others”; check its effect on yourself, the audience, and the context equally.
68. Don’t use social approval, majority, or familiarity as a substitute for the quality of evidence.
69. Don’t label a person psychologically; describe the action being tested, the context, and its consequences.
124. Don’t assume that the interlocutor can see your knowledge, anxiety, or intentions without an explicit message.
125. Don’t attribute someone’s lack of knowledge to incompetence if they might not have had your initial data.
126. Don’t assess others’ attention to you based on your internal sense of being noticed; seek external verification.
127. Don’t attribute a change in behavior solely to external rewards, ignoring motives, environment, and selection.
128. Don’t consider external forces to be the only explanation for your own decision; check the available actions and limitations.
129. Don’t assume you understand someone else’s position better than they do themselves, without verifying their formulation.
