# Calibration, retrospection, provenance and final audit

70. Don’t take a confident tone for accuracy; calibrate confidence with probability, range, and subsequent verification against the outcome.
71. Don’t evaluate the quality of a decision solely based on the result; reconstruct the information and criteria available prior to it.
72. Don’t consider the past obvious after knowing the outcome; compare the retrospective conclusion with the retained forecast.
73. Apply the same requirements to your own hypothesis regarding data and alternatives as you would to someone else’s.
74. Do not retain the conclusion solely for the sake of consistency with the previous statement; correct it when more robust data becomes available.
75. Do not expand the argument to include peripheral, trivial, or symbolic details until the main risk and solution have been verified.
76. Do not collect additional information if you cannot beforehand identify which observation will change the decision.
77. Before the conclusion, check: the question has not been substituted, the alternatives have been considered, the source and uncertainty have been identified, and the conclusion follows from the data.
166. Do not attribute an incorrect source, author, or time to a memory; verify the origin before using it.
167. Do not pass off someone else’s idea as your own due to forgetting the source; record the origin of ideas and quotes.
168. Do not accept a suggested or repeatedly retold memory as independent evidence.
169. Don’t replace access to external search with your own understanding; verify what you find and preserve the key foundations.
170. Don’t keep important information only in your head; keep brief notes on decisions, sources, constraints, and subsequent checks.
